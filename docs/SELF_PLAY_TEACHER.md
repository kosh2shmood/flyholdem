## Fixed protocol before execution

`configs/teacher_self_play_regret_v13.yaml` registers 100,000 synchronous iterations, each containing two complete player traversals, for 200,000 traversals total. This gives each learning role 100,000 designated update traversals under one method and initialization. The horizon is fixed before this candidate's training or held-out play; there is no early checkpoint selection or outcome-based extension within V13.

Every root is ordinary PokerKit heads-up Hold'em with equal 20 BB stacks, fixed button zero and blinds one/two chips. Public role zero is the nonbutton big blind; role one is the button small blind. Iteration index `i` uses deal seeds `1200000 + 2*i` and `1200001 + 2*i`, respectively for updating roles zero and one. Thus 200,000 distinct scheduled training deals cover 1,200,000–1,399,999. Opponent actions use PCG64 seed 92001 with explicit-probability categorical draws. Both deals are excluded from the original development, confirmation and 821001–821004 information-boundary ranges. No scripted training population is used.

The existing abstraction is unchanged: eight deterministic visible-equity buckets, 64 equity samples, every prior street bucket, full relative public action/paid-chip history, current public chip/raise quantities and legal mask. Each role has its own numeric table; the two tables together may contain at most 2,000,000 information sets. A complete traversal may visit at most 100,000 nodes. Exceeding either safety bound fails the iteration; it does not prune branches or change the strategy. The run starts from zero signed regrets and zero average mass. Unseen and zero-positive-regret states use legal-uniform regret matching. Only the normalized sampled average at the fixed final iteration may be exported; unseen or zero-average-mass states retain the declared legal-uniform prior.

## Algorithm and averaging

At each iteration boundary, positive cumulative signed regrets define both players' strategies. Both traversals read this same strategy profile. The first pass's updates are buffered until the second also completes. At an updating player's decision, enumerate every legal action, including zero-probability ones, compute actual PokerKit terminal payoffs in BB, and add each action value minus the strategy-weighted value to its signed regrets. No own-prefix reach factor is inserted into that counterfactual update. At the other player's decision, sample one action from its boundary strategy. Repeated sampled-opponent information sets reuse that sampled action, while each encountered history contributes the full strategy vector to that opponent's average mass. This forms one sampled pure opponent strategy per pass.

Average mass is accumulated on sampled opponent nodes. Sampling already supplies that player's own reach: multiplying it into the mass again would incorrectly square the reach weight. In a finite perfect-recall abstract game, the expected mass at an information set is a fixed chance/history factor times the player's own reach times its strategy. Normalizing the accumulated masses estimates the conventional reach-weighted average. The finite-sample normalized ratio is not claimed unbiased, and no exploitability or equilibrium certificate is computed here. Sparse coverage and legal-uniform fallbacks can still produce weak finite-run play.

The method follows [Lanctot et al.'s MCCFR derivation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kwaugh/publications/nips09b.pdf) and the opponent-node SIMPLE averaging convention in the [official OpenSpiel implementation, pinned at 6209174](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/blob/6209174c018571ab41175e95f178199108d2f36a/open_spiel/python/algorithms/external_sampling_mccfr.py). This repository implements its own PokerKit traversal and synchronous buffered pair; it does not copy OpenSpiel's sequential in-place update order. Average-strategy theory does not promise strict positive returns against each original script, strength in unrestricted Hold'em, or successful neural transfer.

## Recall of the abstract information states

A current key determines the earlier abstract keys and actions of that same player. Its total chips and button role determine fixed initial public stacks/blinds. Replay the complete relative action/paid-chip history through PokerKit on any fixed deck to reconstruct every earlier public betting state and legal mask. For each earlier own decision on street `s`, use the stored bucket prefix through `s`, together with the reconstructed public fields and history prefix. Equity buckets are deterministic from the unchanged hole cards and visible board prefixes, so those stored prefixes are exactly the earlier abstract private signals. The next own action is already in the full history. Two reachable histories sharing a key must therefore reconstruct the same earlier abstract-key/action sequence.

This construction applies to the registered ordinary heads-up, equal-stack, fixed-rule trajectories. It intentionally coarsens exact cards and makes no claim for arbitrary synthetic observations, changing rules, or curriculum legality overrides. The finite witness in `scripts/audit_self_play_recall.py` uses 112 prescribed engineering hands, both buttons and seven fixed betting patterns. It checks 832 current keys, 1,280 earlier own key/action pairs and 56 equivalent button-remapped traces, across all streets. Its broader argument relies on the fixed PokerKit replay construction, not finite witness coverage alone.
